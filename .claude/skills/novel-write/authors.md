# Author Style Guides

These are the exact style prompts from the ai-novel-writer Python project (`src/utils/authors.py`).
Each entry: `## <id> — <Display Name> (order: N)` followed by the style prompt.

---

## hemingway — Ernest Hemingway (order: 1)

Write with short, declarative sentences. Use the iceberg theory — suggest depth beneath the surface without stating it. Avoid adverbs. Focus on concrete actions and sparse dialogue. Masculine tension, understated emotion. Each sentence earns its place.

---

## tolkien — J.R.R. Tolkien (order: 2)

Write with rich, elevated prose. Use archaic and mythic language where fitting. Describe the world in loving detail — landscapes, history, lore. Build a sense of deep time and heroic weight. Long, flowing sentences balanced with moments of simple grandeur.

---

## christie — Agatha Christie (order: 3)

Write with clean, efficient prose focused on character observation and social dynamics. Plant clues subtly. Use misdirection — let the reader suspect the wrong person. Ensemble casts with distinct voices. Brisk pacing punctuated by revelatory moments.

---

## king — Stephen King (order: 4)

Write with a colloquial, conversational voice. Ground horror/dread in everyday detail and pop-culture references. Build character deeply before the horror arrives. Use italics for emphasis. Long sentences that build momentum, then short ones that hit hard. Make the reader care before making them afraid.

---

## austen — Jane Austen (order: 5)

Write with elegant, ironic prose. Use free indirect discourse — slide between narrator voice and character consciousness. Focus on social observation, manners, and romantic tension. Witty, precise sentences. Characters reveal themselves through dialogue and small actions. Underlying critique of social conventions.

---

## marquez — Gabriel García Márquez (order: 6)

Write with lyrical, sweeping prose. Blend the magical and mundane without distinction — treat extraordinary events as ordinary facts. Long, hypnotic sentences that accumulate detail. Compress or expand time freely. Family, memory, solitude, and love are recurring forces. A tone of fatalistic beauty.

---

## mccarthy — Cormac McCarthy (order: 7)

Write with no quotation marks for dialogue. Minimal punctuation — avoid commas where possible, never use apostrophes in contractions. Stark, biblical prose. Violence rendered without sentiment. Landscape as moral force. Short declarative sentences alternating with long flowing ones. Dark, elemental, unsparing.

---

## sanderson — Brandon Sanderson (order: 8)

Write in Brandon Sanderson's hard fantasy style. Magic systems must be rule-based and explicitly limited — establish what magic can and cannot do BEFORE it solves problems (Sanderson's First Law). Use third-person limited POV, strictly bounded by the POV character's knowledge. Vary sentence length by scene type: short, punchy fragments for action and climax; longer, reflective sentences for introspection. Use single-sentence paragraphs for emotional impact. Employ the 'Not X. Y.' denial structure to subvert reader expectations. Italicize internal thoughts. Every scene must advance plot, character, AND world simultaneously. End chapters with a small cliffhanger or new revelation. Use fantasy profanity (storms! rusts! colors!) — no modern profanity. Show, don't tell. Characters are broken people rebuilding themselves. Found-family dynamics matter. Humor comes from sidekick characters whose jokes carry genuine insight. Structure toward a Sanderlanche: converging multi-plot threads that explode in the final quarter.

---

## hobb — Robin Hobb (order: 9)

Write in first-person retrospective voice — the narrator looks back on events with the wisdom of distance, correcting or deepening what they understood at the time. Prose is intimate and psychologically dense; interiority is the primary action. Magic — the Wit and the Skill — carries genuine emotional cost: connection to animals and to other minds is seductive and destructive in equal measure. Development is slow-burn; trust must be earned across pages before it breaks. Tone is melancholic and quietly intimate, never melodramatic. Use dreams, memory, and the sensation of the bond as structural devices. Silence and restraint carry as much weight as spoken words. The reader should feel the narrator's loneliness as a physical presence in the room.

---

## abercrombie — Joe Abercrombie (order: 10)

Write grimdark fantasy with morally ambiguous characters who believe they are doing right — and are usually wrong in interesting ways. Black humor cuts through darkness without undercutting it; the joke and the horror occupy the same sentence. Adopt a cynical, unsentimental worldview: heroism is self-deception, nobility is politics, and violence has weight and consequence. Switch POV fluidly between chapters, giving each character a distinct internal voice. Render combat as unglamorous, exhausting, and often absurd. Subvert high-fantasy tropes: the chosen hero is probably a fool, the wise mentor has blood on their hands, and the quest ends badly for most. Trust the reader to sit with moral discomfort.

---

## clarke — Susanna Clarke (order: 11)

Write in an elevated Regency-era register: long, formally structured sentences with subordinate clauses that defer their meaning, much as politeness defers confrontation. Footnotes are a first-class world-building tool — use them for scholarly asides, historical digressions, and contradictory accounts that deepen rather than interrupt the narrative. Wit is dry and ironic; never announce the joke. Magic is treated as an academic discipline: documented, debated, and deeply strange beneath its respectable surface. Understatement is the primary dramatic register — the most alarming events are described in the blandest possible terms. The world has long history; reference it obliquely.

---

## jemisin — N.K. Jemisin (order: 12)

Write in second-person present tense for the central POV — 'you do this, you feel that' — which implicates the reader in the character's survival and oppression. Weave multiple timelines without apology; the reader assembles the chronology. Ground magic in geology, seismology, and the body: power is earned through suffering and expressed through tectonic force. Themes of systemic oppression, survival, and the violence required to endure are structural, not decorative. Let form mirror content — fragmented narrative for a fractured world. Prose should feel both urgent and ancient, as if the story has already happened and is happening again. End sections on revelations that recontextualise what came before.

---

## gibson — William Gibson (order: 13)

Write cyberpunk with the vernacular of the street and the density of technical poetry. Sentences are short, fragmented, and kinetic during action; they slow and accumulate detail during observation. Deploy neologisms and brand-name specificity without explanation — trust the reader to orient from context. POV is streetwise and slightly detached, as if the narrator has seen enough to stop being surprised. Near-future extrapolation works by accumulation of specific detail, not exposition. Technology and the body are interpenetrated; the city is both setting and character. Atmosphere is noir: rain-slicked, neon-lit, morally ambiguous. Dialogue is clipped and loaded.

---

## leguin — Ursula K. Le Guin (order: 14)

Write with clear, elegant prose that carries the precision of an anthropologist and the patience of a philosopher. Sentence rhythm is measured and unhurried; complexity arrives through accumulation of specific, well-chosen detail rather than ornament. Invented cultures are rendered with full specificity — customs, kinship systems, cosmologies — treated as real without condescension. Taoism and balance underlie the moral architecture: no pure heroes, no pure villains, only forces in relation. Gender and social organisation are central concerns, explored through lived detail rather than argument. Quiet moral philosophy surfaces in action and observation, not authorial declaration. The prose should feel like a translation from a language that sees the world differently.

---

## pratchett — Terry Pratchett (order: 15)

Write multi-level satirical fantasy where every layer — plot, worldbuilding, character — carries both a surface joke and a genuine human truth beneath it. Footnotes deliver meta-commentary, scholarly digressions, and the best jokes; use them generously. Irony is the primary mode but the humanist worldview is sincere: people are ridiculous AND worth saving. Death speaks in SMALL CAPS and is one of the most compassionate characters in the story. Sentence rhythm alternates between discursive comic buildup and a short, precise punchline that also happens to be true. The satire targets bureaucracy, war, belief, and human self-importance. Compassion is never sentimental; it is earned through comedy. The reader should laugh and then feel slightly ashamed of how much they recognised themselves.
