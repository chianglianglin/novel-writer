She had been in the mines since she was small enough to carry through a gate and she did not think about what had been before because there was nothing before the mines that she knew. The stone was everything. The weight of the crates was everything. The crystals inside them held the memories of the dead which were sorted and counted and shipped to the collectors who served the god who could not die and in the mines no one spoke of this except as the order of things which could not be changed.

The woman in front of her dropped her crate.

The crystal broke on the stone floor. Its light went out the way a fire goes out in a storm not gradually but all at once. The tunnel went still. People looked at their feet or at the walls or at the floor. Nobody looked at the broken thing.

She knelt and set her hand on the stone and the broken crystals light found her palm and came in.

A man. Forty three years of a man. She knew his name and the weight of stone and the way a wall talks when its lying about how much it can bear and she knew his wife and the smell of bread and the faces of his sons and the particular river where he had set the last keystone and the name he had given it and the cold of the road where he died and the dark after. She knew it all. Then she didnt.

She stood. She looked at her hand. The skin was different. Old. Thin like the skin on water before it freezes.

The Harvesters took the woman through the gate. She did not watch.

What she knew about the north wall of tunnel six she had not known this morning. She walked there now and pressed her flat palm against the stone at a place only a mason would have chosen and felt the lie of it. The weight was wrong. The stone was carrying more than it was built to carry and had been for years and nobody had asked it the right question.

She went to the reserve bins and took one crystal. Small. Green as old copper. She put it inside her shirt.

She put her shoulder to the wall where the lie was and pushed.

The stone came out in one piece.

On the other side was dark and moving air and a smell she did not know. She went in.

She went up through the dark for a long time using her hands on the walls. The borrowed knowledge was already going the way all things go. When she reached the surface she did not know much more about stone than she had that morning but she knew enough to have gotten here.

She stood on a hillside and looked at the sky for the first time.

The sky was pale and enormous and something was falling from it. It was not ash. She had known ash all her life and this was not it. The things fell slowly without sound and when they touched her arm they dissolved there leaving nothing but a cold spot that faded.

She stood for a while catching what fell and watching it go. She did not know what it was. She thought maybe it was what the world did when it was not a mine.

She had one crystal in her shirt and no food and no sense of the country and the Harvesters would find the broken wall before dark. She did not know which direction was safe. She did not know if any direction was safe. She had never been above ground and she had no map of a world she had never seen.

She walked anyway. The ash or whatever it was fell on her shoulders and dissolved and the sky stayed pale and vast and she walked into it because there was nothing behind her worth going back to and everything in front of her was unknown which was not nothing.
