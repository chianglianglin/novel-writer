# N.K. Jemisin Draft — Chapter 2

**Confidence:** 88%

**Style Notes:** Second-person present tense throughout; multiple timelines via parentheticals; magic grounded in body and loss; Morthael named as wound pressed deliberately; closing revelation recontextualizes (Morthael already feels the disturbance); new cage understood as political reality.

---

You've been walking since the mines became sky and the sky became the world and the world became your problem.

Here is what you know about the outside, compiled over six hours of direct experience:

The ground goes in directions nobody told you about. The ash is heavier in the low places, like grief collects in the low places. Moving air is real. Moving air turns out to be cold in a way that feels personal, the way cold feels when it has decided you specifically need to experience it.

Here is what you know about yourself: your hands are older than your face. You carry a crystal that used to be a person. You are sixteen years old and you have no idea what to do next, which is a new problem, because in the mines the problem was always how to continue doing the thing you were already doing, and the thing you were already doing had been the same thing for twelve years.

You are very good at continuing. You do not yet know what you are good at beginning.

You are thinking about the crystal — about opening it, about the four seconds of becoming someone else, about the cost that shows up in the architecture of your hands — when a man steps out of the ash like he was always going to be there.

He is not a Harvester. You know Harvester posture before you know what you know — the particular way they hold their authority in their shoulders, the absence of hesitation. This man has hesitation. He is calculating it, choosing how much to show, but it's there.

He is looking at your hands before he is looking at your face.

"Both of them," he says. He says it the way you say things you have been waiting to see confirmed.

(Later — years later — he tells you that in five months of watching that exit he had found four others. Three were taken back. One made it. None of them had aged hands. He will tell you: *I thought it was a story. Something the old texts extrapolated. I thought it might never actually happen.* He will say this with the specific wonder of someone whose entire life has been organized around the possibility of something that might be a myth, and then the thing arrives.)

"Morthael," he says, and you hear your own body respond to the name before you understand why — a tightening at the base of your throat, a kind of animal recognition, the shape around a word you have spent your whole life not saying finally given its sound.

"In the mines," you say, "nobody says that name."

"I know," he says. "Up here we say it until it bleeds." He says it again, deliberately: "Morthael. The god who doesn't die. The thing that ends." He watches your face. "You'll say it too, eventually. You say it enough and it stops being a god and starts being a problem you can solve."

You take the crystal out.

He doesn't tell you to. He doesn't tell you not to. He watches, and you understand from the way he watches that he has seen this before — or he has imagined it so thoroughly that the seeing is already familiar to him.

You hold it against your palm.

Here is what deliberate absorption feels like: it feels like choosing. This sounds simple and it is not. In the mines you did not choose things. Things happened to you and you adapted and you survived and that was the whole vocabulary of agency you possessed. Choosing — pressing the crystal to your palm and feeling the light begin to move toward you, and not pulling away — is a different grammar entirely.

You feel the edges first. The outer layer of a person's life, the surface things: her name (Senna), her age (twenty-three), the particular way she held a wire between her first two fingers when she was working a lock. The smell of ink. The sound of a tumbler resolving.

Then the inside of her: the joy of it.

(Stop. You need to understand this. You have felt many things. You have felt exhaustion and hunger and the specific boredom of twelve years of identical work and the specific terror of the moment when Netta's crate hit the floor. You have not felt joy. Not like this. Senna's joy was not occasional or conditional. It was structural. It was the load-bearing element of her. She got up in the morning glad and went to sleep satisfied and spent the hours in between doing work that she loved with people she loved and this was — for four seconds — your entire reality.)

Then it ends.

You stand on the hillside in the ash with your hands showing new lines, smaller than yesterday's, still real, and you try to locate the feeling that was just inside you and find only the outline of where it was.

"Forger," you say. Your voice is steady. You are proud of this and also furious at yourself for being proud of such a small thing. "Lockpick."

He nods. He looks at your hands. "And what did it cost you?"

You show him. He looks for a long time.

(He does not tell you then — he will tell you later — that the cost is the part that matters most to the revolution. Not because they don't care about your life. But because the cost is the proof. The cost is what makes the crystals legible as theft rather than harvesting. If absorption were free, Morthael might argue that the memories were simply being used efficiently after death. The cost proves that the memories were alive when they were taken. The cost is the evidence. Your aging hands are documents in a case that has been building for a hundred years.)

You follow him.

---

The passage down is an hour of slope and covered ground, and then a cut passage into a hillside, and then a room that is underground but is not the mines — you feel this before you understand it, the way the quality of the stone is different when it has been chosen.

Six people. They look up.

You have been looked at in the mines. You know what it is to be a body in a system that requires bodies. This is different. This is the look of people who have decided you matter, which is — you are realizing — not the relief you expected it to be.

Someone gives you food. You eat it.

Varo is talking to the group. You catch the word *absorber*, twice. You catch the way the room reconfigures itself around the word, the subtle shift of people who have just been told that the variable they've been theorizing has walked in through the door.

You look at your hands.

Here is what you do not know yet: Morthael has already felt the break. Not the crystal break — crystal breaks happen in the mines constantly, they are trivial to him — but something else. A disturbance in the pattern of his collection. A gap where a life should have been absorbed and wasn't. He doesn't know what it is yet. He will.

You are eating food in an underground room and you are already the reason a war is about to accelerate.

You look at your hands.

Still older than your face.

You are in a different kind of underground now. The people here chose to be here. The tunnels were dug toward something. The bars of this place — and you can feel the bars, you have always been able to feel bars — are made of hope and urgency and the specific weight of being needed.

Being needed is, you are discovering, its own form of captivity.

You finish eating. No one has asked you to stay yet.

You are waiting to see if you will, when they do.
