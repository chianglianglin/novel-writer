# Susanna Clarke Draft

**Confidence:** 84%

**Style Notes:** Sustained elevated Regency register; four footnotes for world-building and humor (footnote 2 most alarming, delivered blandly; footnote 4 delivers Aldric's personality); understatement as primary dramatic register; long subordinate clauses defer meaning; structural explanation for the wall in parenthetical aside.

---

The practice of memory extraction was, by the time of our story, so thoroughly established in the territories administered by the Immortal Morthael¹ that it had ceased to occasion any particular comment among those employed in the processing facilities, who referred to their work simply as *the sorting* and did not, as a rule, discuss its philosophical implications during their shifts.

She had been employed in the sorting since the age of four or five (the administrative records were, she had been given to understand, somewhat imprecise on this point) and had acquired, in that time, a thorough practical education in the handling of memory crystals, the maintenance of crate integrity during transport, and the social mechanics of a workplace in which one's continued participation was contingent on not dropping anything.²

It was therefore an event of some significance when the woman sorting ahead of her — a person who had, in all previous experience, been entirely reliable in this respect — dropped her crate.

The crystal broke.

Here it is necessary to pause and offer a brief technical description of what happens when a memory crystal breaks, for the benefit of those unfamiliar with the relevant literature.³ A memory crystal, when intact, contains the harvested memories of one deceased individual in a stable, compressed state; the light it produces is blue-white and entirely contained within the crystal's facets. When a crystal breaks, this containment fails, and the memories it holds disperse across the immediate vicinity in a form that is, theoretically, inert — that is to say, the memories are released but not absorbed by anything in particular, and simply dissipate.

*Theoretically.*

She became aware, as she had braced her hand against the floor and the dispersing crystal's light reached her bare palm, that something rather different was occurring.

For a period she estimated at between three and five seconds — during which the Harvesters entered through the gate and the foreman looked up from his ledger and the rest of the sorting line directed its collective attention at the floor in the practiced manner of persons who have considerable experience with not being present at incidents — she was in possession of the complete memories of one Aldric Vann, deceased, who had been a master stonemason of considerable professional distinction.⁴

When this period concluded, she was herself again.

She noted several things: that her hands appeared to have aged by some years; that this was, at a minimum, extremely interesting; and that the north wall of tunnel six, which she had walked past some thousands of times without forming any particular opinion of it, contained a structural weakness that a mason of Aldric Vann's experience would have identified within seconds of observing it and that a person in possession of his memory — briefly, and temporarily — could exploit with one precisely placed push.

She deposited her crate. She collected a small pale crystal from the reserve bins, which she placed inside her clothing for safekeeping, as the alternative seemed inadvisable. She went to tunnel six.

She pushed the wall.

The stone panel — which had been, she now understood, bearing weight it had never been designed to bear, since an administrative decision made sixteen years prior had rerouted a drainage channel in a manner that subtly altered the load distribution throughout the northern tunnel complex — came free with a sound that she would describe as *decisive.*

Behind it was a passage.

She climbed the passage. It was dark, and somewhat irregular, and required rather more exertion than she had anticipated, but she had no particular alternative, and so she climbed until the passage gave way to a slope and the slope gave way to open air and open air gave way to a sky more extensive than she had previously understood skies to be, having had no prior occasion to observe one.

Something was falling from it.

She caught a quantity of this substance on her palm and observed that it dissolved on contact with her skin. She caught more. She stood on the hillside for some time engaged in this experiment.

She was, she noted, outside. She had one crystal in her clothing. The Harvesters would discover the displaced panel before nightfall. She had no provisions, no knowledge of the surrounding territory, and no clear sense of where she ought to go, a deficiency she shared with most people who have recently done something unprecedented.

She began to walk.
