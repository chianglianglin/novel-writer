# William Gibson Draft

**Confidence:** 82%

**Style Notes:** Short kinetic sentences; absorption delivered as "input/inventory" in Gibson's data-processing language; technical specificity throughout; the sky described as the world without name or category; closing reframes her life in detached analytical register. Noir atmosphere via mines as factory-dark.

The crates were ash-cloth wrapped and she moved them and that was the job. Twelve tunnels down, the crystal-glow made everything the wrong shade of blue. You stopped seeing it after a while. You stopped seeing a lot of things. That was how you lasted.

The sorting lines ran six days in seven. She had been on the lines since before she had language for what she was doing, which was processing the compressed experiential data of the dead for upward transit to the collectors. There was a word for what the collectors served but she didn't use it. Nobody used it. The word had weight.

The woman ahead of her — Netta, reliable, good at not drawing attention — lost her grip on the crate.

The crystal hit the stone and the sound it made was the sound of a data compression catastrophically failing, if you wanted a metaphor. She didn't, particularly. The light did things. Wrong colors. Bloom pattern inconsistent with a standard dispersal. She had already braced her hand against the floor and the dispersal found her.

Input.

She ran the inventory later, piecing it together: a man, forty-three years of accumulated expertise in load-bearing structures and the particular behavior of limestone under stress. His name had been Aldric. He'd built something significant once, something that still stood. He had loved his wife in the specific, detailed way that long marriage produces — the knowledge of how another person breathes in sleep, which drawer they always left slightly open, how they sounded at a kettle. He had died cold. His last coherent thought had been about the bread she had pressed into his hands that morning.

She had all of this for four seconds.

Then she was herself, kneeling on the tunnel floor with her hand flat on the stone and the Harvesters already moving through the gate. She pulled her hand back. She stood.

Her hands. The skin across her knuckles was wrong — not injured, just different, like the data had been written at a different resolution than she was running. Older. She filed it.

The north wall of tunnel six was thirty-seven meters in and she had walked it approximately eleven thousand times. She walked it now with new processing parameters. The stone at bracket three was off. Load-bearing mismatch, sixteen years running. One applied force at the correct vector would disengage the panel.

She deposited her crate at the sorting station. She palmed one small crystal from the reserve bins — pale green, unremarkable, standard compression markers. Tucked it against her ribs.

Tunnel six. Bracket three. She put her palm flat and applied the force.

The panel disengaged.

Behind it: moving air. That was new. Air in the mines didn't move. She went through the gap and found the natural passage and climbed it in the dark, using the dead mason's architectural instincts as a navigation system that was already losing signal.

She came up on a hillside.

The sky was the first thing that wasn't what she expected, and she hadn't expected anything. It was larger than could be right. Pale gray, high-contrast, the horizon unresolved and distant in a way that made her want to query it for more data, except there was no query protocol for this, this was just — the world.

Something was falling from it.

She held out her hand. The falling thing landed, soft, cold, dissolved. She held out her hand again. Same result.

She stood there running the experiment until she had a working hypothesis. The sky dropped cold soft things that became water on contact with her skin and she didn't have a name for this process and she was sixteen years old and she had never been outside before and she had one crystal and no food and the Harvesters would find the panel by nightfall.

She started walking. The cold soft things kept falling. The horizon refused to resolve.

She had operated on incomplete data her entire life. This was just a larger dataset.
